# Organization Science computational modeling core reading pack

This pack is designed for the paper on AI recruitment as a feedback institution. It is not a general bibliography. Each paper earns its place by clarifying one part of the causal loop:

```text
Evaluation_t -> Candidate adaptation_t -> Talent pool_t+1 -> Evaluation_t+1
```

## Recommended reading order

| Order | Paper | Why read it now | What to extract personally |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | March 1991 | Learn the canonical logic of endogenous organizational learning | How a very small model generates a long-run theoretical reversal |
| 2 | Levinthal and Schliesmann 2025 | Closest paper to evaluation noise plus adaptive search | Why belief updating and decision rules must be modeled separately |
| 3 | Kane and Alavi 2007 | Learn how to extend March by adding technology mechanisms | How each technological affordance changes a specific learning channel |
| 4 | Fang Lee and Schilling 2010 | Learn how structure preserves useful heterogeneity | Why moderate connectivity can dominate both isolation and full diffusion |
| 5 | Kocak Levinthal and Puranam 2023 | Learn how a model introduces a second organizational problem | How search and coordination are varied independently to reveal a boundary condition |

## How to use the cards

1. Read the one-sentence theory move before opening the paper.
2. While reading, fill only the blocks marked `Your reading fill-in`.
3. Record page, figure, table, or equation locations rather than copying long passages.
4. After reading, rewrite Section 02 in your own words without looking at the card.
5. Finish by deciding whether the paper supplies a mechanism, boundary condition, contrast, or modeling style for our project.

## Shared terminology ledger

| Canonical term | Meaning in this pack | Avoid confusing with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| exploration | search or experimentation involving uncertain alternatives | any change in behavior |
| exploitation | refinement or persistence around known alternatives | simple organizational inertia |
| belief updating | revising estimated merits from feedback | the decision rule that selects an action |
| evaluation feedback | information generated after alternatives or actors are assessed | objective ground truth |
| organizational adaptation | change in beliefs or actions that affects performance over time | one-period prediction accuracy |
| candidate adaptation | applicants changing skill investment or evidence production in response to evaluation | employer-side model updating |
