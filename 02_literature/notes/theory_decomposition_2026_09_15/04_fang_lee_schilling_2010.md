# Fang, Lee and Schilling (2010) — Isolation of Subgroups and Organizational Learning

**Theory move.** The paper changes the structure through which knowledge diffuses and finds that moderate subgroup connectivity can preserve variation while sharing superior ideas. [Publisher record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.1090.0468), *Organization Science* 21(3):625–642. **Coverage:** publisher abstract and [earlier card](../orgsci_core_reading_pack/04_fang_lee_schilling_2010.md); network equations, figures and exceptions await full text.

1. **Phenomenon.** Organizational learning benefits from sharing useful knowledge but may converge too quickly [verified: abstract framing].
2. **Existing theory / assumption.** More communication need not improve learning monotonically; treating diffusion separately from preservation of heterogeneity obscures a tension [analysis based on abstract].
3. **Why it matters.** Dense connections transmit good ideas and can remove independent exploration; isolation retains variation but prevents diffusion [analysis].
4. **Mechanism.** Semi-isolated subgroups and cross-group ties change learning pathways [verified: abstract]. Agent beliefs, exact topology, update order and payoff representation are **not assessable**.
5. **Design / result.** The abstract reports a connectivity comparison with moderate intergroup links yielding high equilibrium performance, and discusses problem complexity, dynamism and personnel turnover as contingencies. Precise manipulation, baseline, isolation and robustness ranges are **not assessable**.
6. **Contribution / limit.** Structure can balance diffusion and diversity in the simulated organization; there is no direct result about common AI guidance or applicants.
7. **Relation: boundary hypothesis.** Standardized feedback could act as an institutional broadcast that aligns investments while narrowing skill variety.
