# Kocak, Levinthal and Puranam (2023) — Search and Coordination

**Theory move.** The paper crosses search difficulty with coordination difficulty and shows that hierarchical influence can help organizational agility when both challenges occur, even without superior leader knowledge. [Publisher HTML](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2022.1601), *Organization Science* 34(2):851–869; [earlier card](../orgsci_core_reading_pack/05_kocak_levinthal_puranam_2023.md). **Coverage:** substantial official text; supplement and exact equation/replication inventory pending.

1. **Phenomenon.** Individually promising choices can fail to combine under interdependent tasks [verified: task-environment framework].
2. **Existing theory / assumption.** Search-only settings vary payoff landscapes but can leave coordination trivial; pure-coordination settings can suppress search difficulty [verified: framework/Table 1].
3. **Why it matters.** A flat-versus-hierarchical comparison can change meaning across task environments. Expertise, authority and influence are also separable features of hierarchy [verified: discussion/endnote].
4. **Mechanism.** Adaptive individuals choose actions; payoff variability governs search difficulty and interdependence coordination difficulty; influence networks change how beliefs converge. Individual and collective payoffs are positively correlated, abstracting from cooperation conflicts [verified: Model Structure]. The exact action/belief equations and period count await a targeted audit.
5. **Design / result.** Orthogonal task dimensions and influence structures isolate the setting where hierarchical influence favors rapid satisfactory performance. It does not make hierarchy universally superior; agility and eventual performance can be distinct criteria [verified: Table 1/Results/Discussion]. Full robustness settings remain pending.
6. **Contribution / limit.** Organizational form works differently when finding actions and making them jointly viable are both hard. The result concerns belief influence, not administrative hierarchy as a whole.
7. **Relation: contrast and possible boundary.** Candidates compete for limited job capacity, so one person's adaptation changes others' opportunity. This is **our inferred market-coordination analogy**, not an author claim. Before adding agents, test whether capacity coupling alone changes R2/R3 effects under varying role scarcity; specify candidate and employer payoff conflicts explicitly.
