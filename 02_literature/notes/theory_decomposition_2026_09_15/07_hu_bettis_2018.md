# Hu and Bettis (2018) — Multiple Organization Goals with Feedback

**Theory move.** The paper relaxes the single high-level goal in performance-feedback theory and examines how shared task environments make feedback on multiple operational goals interdependent and hard to attribute. [Publisher record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.2018.1207), *Organization Science* 29(5):873–889. **Coverage:** publisher abstract and metadata; identification details, tables and limitations are **not assessable**.

1. **Phenomenon.** Organizations use goal feedback to adapt, yet operational goals can share technological tasks [verified: abstract].
2. **Existing theory / assumption.** Much feedback research examines one overall goal and misses lower-level operational interdependence [authors' characterization in abstract].
3. **Why it matters.** A change in one goal may contain information about other shared tasks, confounding learning and credit assignment [verified: abstract; exact causal structure pending].
4. **Mechanism / method.** Feedback on three strategic operational goals with shared technological environments is analyzed by panel vector autoregression in the automobile industry [verified: abstract]. This is **not a simulation**; agents, state-update equations and ABM seed policy are inapplicable. PVAR specification and empirical unit await full text.
5. **Result.** Interdependent feedback can create severe, misleading confusion in learning from goals [verified: abstract]. The magnitude, signs and conditions cannot be reported from current coverage.
6. **Contribution / limit.** Operational task coupling qualifies single-goal feedback reasoning. The paper supplies no direct evidence on applicant responses.
7. **Relation: contrast.** Our outcomes—conditional fit, placement, learning, evidence conversion and resource gap—need separate definitions. **Analysis:** a common feedback signal could improve one measure while worsening another. Avoid treating an aggregate hiring score as proof of all goals; testing cross-goal credit assignment would require a new, specified mechanism.
