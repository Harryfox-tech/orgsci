## Systematic perspective reading — not an experimental paper

**Theory-level use.** The paper is a perspective in a complexity-themed *Organization Science* issue, not a computational experiment with its own parameter table. Its abstract argues for studying adaptive agents, their schemata, interactions, recombination and organizational architecture rather than inferring aggregate behavior from a representative actor ([publisher record](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/orsc.10.3.216)). The non-obvious organizational claim we can safely draw at abstract coverage is methodological: local rationality and local adjustment do not guarantee a simple, monotone collective trajectory. Exact wording of its comparison to earlier organization theory needs the main text.

**Causal architecture (our reconstruction, not a reported Anderson simulation).** Agent schema guides local action; action and network interaction change information and adaptation opportunities; these changes can alter the fitness landscape that guides subsequent action. Architecture and landscape can themselves become managerial levers. This is compatible with our `evaluation → candidate response → talent-pool composition → next evaluation` feedback institution, but the paper's abstract does not specify our actors, skill stocks, ranking rules, employer capacity or performance measures. Treating it as direct evidence for such mechanisms would be a citation error.

**Why “Kane-level” dimensions are inapplicable.** There is no paper-specific finite-agent model to audit for synchronous updates, baseline parameter values, paired seeds, mediator time series, short/long outcome reversals, combinations or sensitivity analyses. A rigorous card therefore states the theoretical proposition and its limits, not manufactured simulation details. This source belongs in a *modeling orientation* category, outside the core evidentiary chain for a recruitment mechanism or an ABM result.

**Transfer test (our analysis, unrun).** The perspective motivates one clean ablation: compare an exogenous evaluation regime with an otherwise identical regime that lets candidate skill/evidence responses alter the next-period pool. Hold initial candidate stocks and employer demand matched; measure pool composition, evaluation distributions and organization-level allocation. If adding the feedback loop produces no change in these mediators, merely calling the model “complex adaptive” contributes little. No such comparison is reported by Anderson, and the locked baseline is unchanged.
